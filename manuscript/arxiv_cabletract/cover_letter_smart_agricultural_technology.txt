Smart Agricultural Technology

Dear Editors,

Re: Submission of "CableTract: A Co-Designed Cable-Driven Field Robot for
Low-Compaction, Off-Grid Capable Agriculture — A Prototype-Free Feasibility
Framework and Analytical Envelope"

I am pleased to submit the above manuscript for consideration as an original
research article in Smart Agricultural Technology.

The paper presents an open, reproducible computational framework that integrates
physics-based modelling, closed-loop control simulation, and techno-economic
analysis to assess the feasibility of CableTract — a two-module, cable-driven
autonomous field machine in which a stationary winch and a lighter helical-pile
anchor tension a cable across a strip, while a lightweight (~250 kg) implement
carriage performs the field operation. The heavy bodies stay on the headland and
only the carriage enters the field. The central methodological contribution is a
co-designed implement library — sized for the cable architecture rather than
borrowed from conventional tractors — together with a single reproducible
pipeline that couples cable and anchor mechanics, ASABE D497.7 draft modelling,
an hourly solar/wind/battery energy simulator over six climate sites, a
field-coverage planner, a contact-pressure compaction model, discounted-cash-flow
economics with life-cycle CO2, and a global sensitivity analysis. Five
higher-fidelity studies — a nonlinear p-y anchor model, a closed-loop
depth-control co-simulation (PID and model-predictive control), a cable-safety
model, a soil-tool discrete-element model, and a co-design robustness sweep —
stress-test the load-bearing assumptions and quantify the dominant engineering
risks.

Relevance to Smart Agricultural Technology. The work aligns with the journal's
scope — the integration of advanced computing with control systems for
agricultural production. It contributes (i) investigator-developed software and
control models, including a closed-loop depth-control co-simulation and an open
analysis pipeline, rather than the application of off-the-shelf tools; (ii) a
quantitative assessment of on-farm energy efficiency and socio-economic viability
(net present value, payback, and life-cycle CO2) under smart, electrified,
off-grid-capable operation; and (iii) a soil-preparation and field-robotics
machine concept directed at the societal challenges of soil health and
agricultural decarbonisation. All code, data, and figures are openly available,
so every reported number is independently reproducible.

I wish to be transparent on two points. First, this is a prototype-free
feasibility study: the results derive from first-principles models built on
experimentally calibrated standards (for example ASABE D497.7, and published
helical-pile lateral-capacity and tractor contact-pressure measurements) and are
validated against analytic limits, and the manuscript explicitly enumerates the
mechanical risks that a prototype phase must resolve. Second, an earlier version
of this work is available as a preprint (arXiv:2604.09938); the present
submission is a substantially revised and extended version that adds the five
higher-fidelity verification studies described above. Posting of the preprint is
consistent with the journal's submission policy and does not constitute prior
publication.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under
consideration by any other journal. It is single-authored; I declare no competing
interests, and the research received no specific grant from funding agencies in
the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors. The complete analytical code
and data are openly available at https://github.com/yilmazozgur/cabletract.

Thank you for considering this submission. I look forward to the reviewers'
comments.
